# Research Abstract (366 words)

## Indian Banking-Stock Risk, Monetary Policy, and Macroeconomic Conditions

**Study period:** January 2010–December 2025 (descriptive equity data); February 2015–December 2025 (monthly macro regressions).

This study integrates bank-specific equity risk analysis with macro-financial evidence on India's banking sector. It asks how State Bank of India (SBI), HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and Axis Bank differ in monthly returns and volatility; whether their equity returns co-move with the NIFTY 50; and how observed returns relate to Reserve Bank of India (RBI) repo-rate conditions, India VIX and the USD/INR exchange rate. It also combines an earlier three-instrument repo-rate study with a daily examination of recorded RBI policy-rate effective dates and a COVID-era sensitivity analysis.

The descriptive dataset comprises 192 monthly returns per instrument from January 2010 through December 2025. The aligned monthly macroeconomic regressions span February 2015 through December 2025, with 131 observations per bank. Three ordinary least-squares specifications are estimated on matched samples using Newey-West heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent standard errors with three lags. A separate event-window exercise covers 26 repo-rate effective-date changes (18 cuts and eight hikes) during 2015–2025 and reports raw bank and index returns over event-day, three-trading-day and eleven-trading-day windows. Robustness checks exclude March–December 2020, leaving 121 monthly observations per bank.

At the individual-stock level, ICICI Bank records the highest average monthly return (1.5755%) and SBI the highest monthly standard deviation (9.7276%), while NIFTY 50 volatility is lower (4.7107%). Bank–market correlations range from 0.6774 to 0.7783. In the contemporaneous expanded regressions, NIFTY 50 returns are positively associated with returns for all four banks. SBI's positive repo-rate-change coefficient (7.1022; p=0.0065) and HDFC Bank's negative USD/INR coefficient (-0.7329; p=0.0019) remain nominally significant after excluding the designated 2020 months. In contrast, the original ICICI VIX association and several repo-rate associations lose nominal significance. The earlier effective-date study reports a mean SBI three-day raw return of -1.1074% (p=0.0288) and a bank-minus-index difference of -0.8036 percentage points (p=0.0272), without identifying unexpected monetary-policy shocks.

Overall, the evidence supports strong common-market co-movement alongside bank-specific and sometimes sample-sensitive macro-financial associations. The results are observational, are subject to multiple-testing and data-vendor limitations, and neither identify causal RBI policy effects nor establish out-of-sample predictability. The project provides a reproducible foundation for subsequent announcement-surprise, balance-sheet and inflation research.

**Keywords:** Indian bank equities; NIFTY 50; RBI repo rate; India VIX; USD/INR; event-window returns; HAC estimation; sensitivity analysis.
