# Abstract (262 words)

This study investigates how bank-specific equity risk in India relates to monetary policy and wider macro-financial conditions. It examines monthly stock returns for State Bank of India (SBI), HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and Axis Bank alongside the NIFTY 50 benchmark. Descriptive analysis covers January 2010 to December 2025 (192 observations per series), while regressions use 131 aligned months from February 2015 to December 2025. The study combines descriptive statistics, correlations, and ordinary least squares regressions with Newey-West heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent standard errors. Explanatory variables include the RBI repo-rate level and its monthly change, NIFTY 50 returns, India VIX, and monthly USD/INR changes. An additional event-window analysis examines 26 recorded RBI repo-rate effective-date changes, and a sensitivity test excludes March to December 2020. Across the descriptive sample, ICICI Bank has the highest average monthly return (1.58%), whereas SBI has the highest monthly return volatility (9.73%). All four banks are more volatile than the NIFTY 50. Bank returns show consistently positive associations with the market benchmark, with correlations ranging from 0.68 to 0.78. In the expanded regressions, SBI's repo-rate-change coefficient is positive, while HDFC Bank's USD/INR coefficient is negative; both remain statistically significant when the selected 2020 months are excluded. Other macro-financial associations are weaker or sensitive to sample selection. In the original event-window analysis, SBI records a negative average three-day return around RBI effective dates, although the analysis does not isolate unexpected policy announcements. Overall, market-wide movements exhibit the most consistent relationship with bank-stock returns, while additional associations vary across banks. The results describe historical patterns rather than establishing causality or reliable forecasts.
